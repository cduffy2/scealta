The Salmon of Knowledge

The Well

Before the world was as it is, there was a deep well of water.

The Well of Segais was hidden away near Carbury in County Kildare, where the rivers of Ireland were said to rise. The river Boyne came out of it first. Around the well grew nine hazel trees, and every autumn their nuts dropped into the water. Inside every nut was a share of all that was ever known.

The First Man

Fintan mac Bóchra had been alive since before the flood.

He came to Ireland with the first people ever to set foot on the island. After the great flood rose, Fintan was the only one to survive. A feat he had managed by changing his shape first into an eagle, then a hawk, before finally assuming the form of a salmon. Fintan swam up to the Well of Segais, and there he ate nine nuts, one from each of the hazel trees that surrounded the well. With the ninth nut, he had the knowledge of the world inside him, and away down the river Boyne he went.

The Poet

It was said that anyone who ate this salmon would gain all the wisdom of the world.

Finegas, a druid-poet, had given his life to this prophecy. He built his house on the bank of the river Boyne and fished every morning, trying to catch the legendary salmon of knowledge. Over time, a young boy named Fionn came to live and study with Finegas, as he hid from the men who had killed his father.

The Catch

Then one morning, the water moved differently.

Finegas felt it before he could see it, a weight on the line unlike any other. He reeled in the fish, silver and glistening. He handed the fish to Fionn, told him to cook it carefully, and not to eat any of it. Then he went to rest, certain his long wait was finally over.

The Thumb

The fish crackled over the fire as its skin turned golden brown.

As Fionn turned the fish over the fire, a blister developed on its skin. It grew bigger and bigger until finally it burst, spraying fat on Fionn’s thumb. Without thinking, he put his thumb to his mouth. When he brought the salmon to Finegas, the old man saw the boy’s eyes glowing like two fires. Finegas grew quiet. He asked him about the world, about things no boy could know. The boy answered every one.

The Teacher

Finegas sighed, recognising his fate. Like all teachers, he must one day be surpassed by his student.

Finegas did not fight it. He gave the boy the rest of the fish. Fionn grew up to lead the Fianna, the greatest warriors in Ireland. In the end, Finegas understood that passing on what he knew was the same as having it.
